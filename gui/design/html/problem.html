Optimal Control Problem in Thermostatics

Consider the optimal control problem for the thermostatics process occurring in a homogeneous rod of length \(l=1\) with a given temperature at the left (\(x=0\)) and right (\(x=l\)) ends. The goal is to control the temperature of the internal heat sources in order to bring the temperature distribution \(u(x)\) in the rod as close as possible to the desired distribution \(\phi(x)\).

The mathematical formulation of the problem is to minimize the functional:

\[ J(v) = \int_0^l (u(x;v) - \phi(x))^2 \, dx \]

subject to the following conditions

\[ Lu = f(x) + v(x), \quad x \in (0,l), \]
\[ u(0) = g_0, \quad u(l) = g_l, \]

where \(g_0, g_l\) are given constants, \(\phi(x)\) is a specified function of the desired temperature distribution in the rod, \(f(x)\) is a given function describing the effect of internal heat sources, \(v \equiv v(x)\) is the control function to be determined for the internal heat sources, \(u \equiv u(x) \equiv u(x;v)\) is the state function (temperature) influenced by the control \(v(x)\) over the interval \([0,l]\).

The action of the differential operator \(L\) in the corresponding equation is defined by

\[ Lu = -\frac{d}{dx} \Big( k(x) \cdot \frac{du}{dx} \Big) = -\big( k(x) \cdot u'(x) \big)', \]

where \(k(x)\) is the variable thermal conductivity coefficient along the rod, satisfying the condition:

\[ k(x) \ge c_1, \quad x \in (0,l), \]

where \(c_1 > 0\) is a given positive constant.
